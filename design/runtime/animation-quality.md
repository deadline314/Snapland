# Animation quality research

## Scope and current baseline

Read `design/runtime/eight-direction-walk.md` and the current character renderer notes. The player already has eight separately authored directions and eight full-body walk cels per direction (64 poses); actual horizontal travel controls the phase, and a shared source-space foot anchor is fitted to `FEET_BASELINE = 346` on a 280×360 render canvas. Four cardinal action poses remain separate. NPC activity currently uses four frames for gestures, dance and a short walk. The renderer presents the composed image on a `Sprite3D` in the 3D park with a custom shader.

The next quality gain should come from distinct readable poses and consistent foot contact, not from speeding up the same cels or dissolving between images. Each authored direction must keep the whole character and identity in frame: face, hair streak, outfit, equipment and both feet. Keep palette, camera scale, proportions and the common foot-contact row stable. Do not mirror or rotate a view to manufacture another direction.

## Trade-offs

| Approach | Motion and style | Cost and main failure mode | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 distinct hand-drawn poses for a one-second walk loop (nominal 16 drawings/s) | Adds useful breakdowns between contact/down/pass/up; preserves authored contour, facial readability and costume detail exactly. | Twice the current player cels: 128 full-body poses for eight directions. Each pose needs cleanup and consistency review. | Best next step. Pilot one representative direction with the existing 8 landmarks plus meaningful breakdowns, then replicate the process direction by direction. Keep phase distance-driven; 16 is the nominal authored sample count, not a timer. |
| 24 distinct hand-drawn poses per second | Can represent smaller changes in timing and weight. | 50% more drawings than 16/s across every view and appearance variant. Extra cels may add labor without visible benefit at the camera's actual character size; more detailed cels also increase identity drift risk. | Reserve for close-up/cinematic actions or a measured need after the 16-pose pilot. Do not make 24 a blanket target. |
| Continuous 2D bone/mesh animation with weights and IK | Smooth fractional motion; IK can keep feet/hand targets planted and retiming keys is cheap. | Mesh deformation changes contours continuously, can stretch detailed features, and needs per-view topology/weights and draw-order repair. Eight viewpoints make setup and review multiply. | Prototype only on torso/limbs or secondary motion. Keep face, hair silhouette and distinctive outfit features on authored attachments/poses; quantize or bake if interpolation visibly smears the intended art. |
| 3D rig animation baked to 2D frames | Consistent volume and pose timing across directions; easy to adjust keys and regenerate frame sequences. Can emit full-body cels for the existing `Sprite3D` path. | Requires a matching model, materials, eight-camera/view policy, lighting and bake tooling; raw renders may look too plastic or drift from the painterly source. A rig does not guarantee the desired silhouette or identity. | Strong optional authoring aid if an approved model/material pipeline already exists. Treat output as pose/reference material and hand-correct frames; avoid replacing current art with an unreviewed raw bake. |

The commonly cited 24 fps teaching example is an animation convention, not a requirement for 24 unique gameplay images each second. Keep movement deterministic: accumulate actual travel, map the stride phase to the authored pose count, and hold the current pose while stopped. Tune the distance-per-cycle against the visible footfall rhythm. If playback samples between cels, switch discretely at pose boundaries; do not alpha-crossfade full bodies, which creates a double silhouette and an unauthored pose.

## Recommended implementation sequence

1. Keep the current eight-direction, full-body atlas format and travel-driven clock. For one side-facing direction, create a 16-pose pilot representing two steps. Place both contacts, downs, passings and ups first, then add breakdowns that change silhouette and weight transfer; do not spend new frames on duplicates. Use onion skin across the loop boundary as well as adjacent frames.
2. Review the pilot as a small on-screen `Sprite3D` at the actual park camera scale. Check that the foot planted at contact remains visually fixed through stance, the opposite arm swings, hips/shoulders counter-rotate, head/body rise and fall are coherent, and the face, cream streak, shoes, satchel and costume stay recognizable in every pose. Adjust timing/holds from observed travel speed; no transparent blend between poses.
3. Once the pilot reads clearly, use the same landmark sheet, palette, camera scale and identity checklist for the other seven directions. Keep direction changes on the existing sector selection; do not blend between views. Keep idle/actions authored as separate states.
4. Give NPCs the same pose review standard. Four frames remain suitable for compact held gestures; use at least the meaningful contact/down/pass/up landmarks for a walking loop instead of reusing a four-frame gesture cadence as locomotion.

## Pose-review evidence and current pilot

The reference image is useful for naming and comparing contact, down, passing and up landmarks, but it is not an acceptance template whose frame count guarantees a good gait. In the main session's visual review of the first 16-pose pilot, the first-row order read unnaturally despite having 16 distinct poses; frames 5 and 13 did not show enough knee bend in passing, and same-leg progression did not connect clearly. This is a concrete failure of “more cels = better animation.” The second revision is being shaped around the landmark sequence and the reference image. It remains a work in progress until the revised loop is visually reviewed; this document does not claim that the revision passes.

Use a two-part gate for each direction: (1) inspect the numbered full-body poses against the contact/down/pass/up order for both alternating steps, including the loop boundary, planted-foot progression, leg bend, counter-swing and stable character identity; (2) inspect the sequence at intended on-screen size and playback speed, then slow it down for pose continuity. A distinctive silhouette in every cell is insufficient if the order or weight transfer reads wrong. Check the whole frame and ground contact; do not accept a cropped foot, an identity-changing face/hair/outfit, or an alpha crossfade that doubles the silhouette. A still-sheet review is not a runtime/GPU result, and a reference image is not gameplay evidence.

## Foot-to-ground alignment

The documented renderer fits source art to a shared pixel foot baseline, but world placement is a separate coordinate. Treat the actor root/ground contact as the world-space foot plane, and position the entire untrimmed sprite card so the authored baseline lands on that plane. Keep the card's transparent padding below/around the feet; do not crop the lowest visible shoe pixels or let a ground-plane shader clip them. Keep any contact-shadow disc centered on the actor's ground root with a small vertical separation to avoid z-fighting. Verify the baseline at the actual camera angle and on sloped/uneven ground; headless anchor checks cannot prove the rendered shoes are not cut off. No alpha fade is needed to correct contact.

This is a research recommendation only. No generated images, GPU visual review, or gameplay test was performed here.

## Rejected native rig trial

The root session subsequently generated one LEFT-profile parts atlas and rendered a five-piece torso/thigh/shin rig in the real park. The headless math checks and native capture fixture completed, but actual inspection exposed unnatural leg proportions, visible circular knee joints and shoes rotating with the shins. The first fixture also put the subject behind the fountain; it was relocated to open ground before judging the full body. The user explicitly rejected the resulting appearance as grotesque on 2026-10-12. These failures override any successful numerical test.

The runtime rig, parts atlas, manifest and trial tests were withdrawn and archived locally. The accepted full-body avatar remains in use. [Rejected native appearance](../../pm/Snapland-art/images/ART-16-rejected-rig.png) is retained only as failure evidence. The trial does not establish fluid walking, planted feet in world coordinates, eight-view support, or production-ready character animation. Future animation must preserve the approved full-body proportions and be reviewed as a complete cycle before adoption.
