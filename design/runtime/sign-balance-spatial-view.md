# SG-23 spatial sign-view candidate

## Presentation contract

This candidate replaces the flat sign illustration with a **true 3D scene and 2D HUD**. A fixed, low three-quarter `Camera3D` renders a dedicated `World3D` through a `SubViewport`; the physical sign, supports, axle, counterweight rail, hanging mass and chain, pennant, dock, water, and directional-light shadows are `MeshInstance3D`/3D scene nodes. The existing paper cards, target-angle gauge, status, and pointer rail remain 2D overlays. This is not an all-3D interface claim.

The focal object is a counterweighted harbor sign mounted on a fixed brass axle. The sign body and rail turn together from the existing snapshot angle; the forged mass slides along that rail from `balance`, and its short chain follows the mass. A separate pennant shows the snapshot wind direction and strength. No view-side timer, scoring, auto-correction, or invented result state is introduced. The camera and horizon stay fixed so the board's motion remains the signal.

The harbor scene uses broad cedar deck boards, darker edge timbers, a braced iron-and-brass support, a layered sign face, a real shaft and hubs, and a small amount of dock hardware. The restrained sea-glass water plane and phase-colored sky establish the site around that machine; no generic 2D illustration is placed over the scene. The existing HUD stays in the same safe area. The moved stage card is narrow and left-aligned in the top row; the wind forecast sits on the right, leaving the sign's projected swing clear between them.

Time phase changes the 3D environment and key-light direction/color, so the moving sign casts its shadow under the current light. Reduced motion removes no physical feedback: sign angle, mass position, rail, forecast arrow, wind pennant direction, and shadow remain visible. The pennant has no separate idle flutter. Geometry, nodes, and materials are created once; per-frame updates change transforms and existing material/light properties from the snapshot.

## Source decisions

The diversity survey already records nine first-party game sources. This pass directly opened official text pages for seven games and reused only those written descriptions. I did not inspect source screenshots, gameplay stills, or videos; the linked image/video collections were not used to infer these choices.

| First-party source text | Decision for this candidate |
|---|---|
| [Nintendo: WarioWare: Get It Together!](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/warioware-get-it-together-switch/) describes very short microgames and different character abilities. | Give this game a single physical counterweight mechanism that explains its particular control; do not import character swaps or its fast comic pacing. |
| [Panic / Playdate: b360](https://play.date/games/b360/) describes brick-breaking with a rotational twist and D-pad/crank control. | Let the object's actual mechanical relation define the feedback: the mass moves on a rail, and the sign turns about its axle. Do not copy its space setting. |
| [Annapurna Interactive: Donut County](https://annapurnainteractive.com/games/donut-county) calls its game a physics puzzle played as an expanding hole. | Make the visible physical rule carry the stage's spectacle and state changes; do not borrow the hole or swallowing action. |
| [Drool: Thumper](https://thumpergame.com/) describes rhythm-action through speed and physicality and identifies both 2D and VR presentations. | Make force, resistance, and settling perceptible through motion tied to model state. Keep this harbor instrument calm; do not adopt speed, violence, or its track camera. |
| [Lucas Pope: Return of the Obra Dinn prerelease notes](https://obradinn.com/media/prerelease.html) describes a fixed low-resolution, two-color style and alternate output options for comfort. | Keep the camera fixed and the mechanical silhouette/readouts separable; do not imitate its monochrome treatment or resolution. |
| [House House / Panic: Untitled Goose Game](https://untitled.goose.game/) gives a concise playable fantasy and links a screenshot set. | No visual form is inferred from this text-only read; its screenshots were not viewed, so it contributes no art or composition rule here. |
| [Lucas Pope / Panic: Mars After Midnight](https://play.date/games/mars-after-midnight/) describes a work-simulation premise and a deliberately imprecise crank. | Keep continuous adjustment broad and forgiving: the existing broad rail remains the pointer target, while the physical weight makes the adjustment readable. |

The mechanics and harbor materials come from the SG-23 ticket/design, not these game pages. Research only informs the separation between distinct verbs and their visual feedback; no source scene or asset is reproduced.

## Godot implementation references

- [Godot Viewport documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_viewport.html) describes a viewport as an independent 2D/3D view and render target. The candidate uses `SubViewportContainer` as the scene output, with the 2D HUD drawn above it.
- [Godot CanvasItem documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_canvasitem.html) specifies relative Z accumulation, tree draw order, and `show_behind_parent`. The stage keeps relative Z at zero and draws behind only its own presentation parent. With the existing session child order, the canvas draws the opaque session background, stage texture, local paper overlays, and later HUD sibling in that order. A negative stage Z would put the texture behind the session background and hide the actual apparatus. The stage ignores pointer input; the existing HUD sibling retains its button routing.
- [Godot SubViewport-as-texture tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/using_viewport_as_texture.html) demonstrates routing a 3D scene through a `SubViewport` texture. Here the `SubViewportContainer` owns that display path.
- [Godot Camera3D documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_camera3d.html) documents orthographic projection and `unproject_position()`. The camera stays orthographic/fixed; projected test bounds come from the transformed sign mesh corners.
- [Godot DirectionalLight3D documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/classes/class_directionallight3d.html) describes a directional light and its shadow mode. The scene uses its real shadow map for the sign/support on the dock and changes light direction by phase.
- [Godot 3D lighting and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html) documents directional/contact shadow behavior. The candidate relies on ordinary directional shadows, not a fake offset ellipse.

## Verification boundary

The isolated view regression checks actual node types, camera, shadow-enabled light, transformed sign bounds and overlay clearance, model-to-angle and model-to-weight transforms, wind direction, day-phase light changes, stable scene-node count, reduced-motion retention, and the unchanged pointer-rail signal mapping. The existing pure sign model remains unchanged.

The candidate headless view run reports 80 checks passed with an isolated save path: all 72 earlier checks remain, plus eight actual Session/HUD feedback checks. The view suite retains the prior forecast/calm, negative and positive wind directions, all day palettes, normal and reduced-motion feedback, angle/shadow mapping, title fit, HUD/gauge/control-row safety, and maximum-tilt clipping assertions, expressed against the new 3D projection where the old flat-board pixel bounds no longer apply. It adds actual SubViewport, mesh, camera/shadow, transformed bounds, weight/pennant forces across all seven model gusts, day-phase lighting, and stable-node checks. Its canvas-order check now requires the stage to share the session's canvas Z and draw behind its own parent; the old negative-Z implementation fails this regression. The feedback checks failed on the former Session placement before the sign-only correction. The pure model remains unchanged.

The native fixture uses the existing park launch and actual mouse, pause/retry controls, and seven-stage keyboard route; it asserts that live model snapshots move the real mass and sign. At both launches and at the stage-three/stage-six captures, it waits for `RenderingServer.frame_post_draw`, reads the SubViewport texture and final main-viewport texture from that completed frame, and samples the central mechanism region below the transient feedback bar and above the paper controls. Both images must have nonflat RGB ranges and at least 85% of central samples must match the actual source texture within a small RGB tolerance. This catches an empty render target and a rendered apparatus hidden by an opaque ancestor background. At those checks, any visible actual contextual feedback must also clear the projected live board, title and mass, and all local paper cards. No feedback or model result is injected by the native fixture. The live session/HUD canvas order is also required, and the existing mouse drag, pause/resume, retry and practice checks still exercise real button routing. The fixture has a 300-second wall-clock watchdog, with Root retaining a separate external 360-second guard. This bounded capture budget comes from Root's measured normal run (41.45 seconds of movie output taking about 47 seconds of wall time); quarter-speed output is projected to take about 184 seconds of wall time and could exceed the former 180-second limit. No model, result, input, or rendering assertion is relaxed by this timeout adjustment.

Root independently reran the final view suite (80 checks) and the unchanged model suite (48 checks). Normal native capture completed 2,487 frames at 60 FPS (41.45 seconds); quarter-speed capture completed 9,736 frames at 60 FPS (162.266667 seconds). Both exited zero with no script errors, ERROR or FAIL lines. Each run checked six actual source/final rendered frames: 514–515 of 518 central samples matched, and all four visible contextual feedback checks cleared the live sign, title, mass and local cards. Both reached seven-stage formal 700-point results through real keyboard input, and the real pointer/pause/retry/reduced-motion practice route retained private progress. Root inspected all six screenshots from each run. Root played both actual movies to their natural ended state and inspected intermediate rendered views; the ticket retains the two movies and both six-image contact sheets. These native routes use the noon phase; the other three lighting phases remain CPU configuration checks, not visually accepted native captures. The earlier invisible-stage capture remains rejected. This bounded route does not establish complete free play, all settings/device combinations, or final art quality.
