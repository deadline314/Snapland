# Native island NPC motion review

`game/tests/native_island_npc_motion_review.tscn` loads the real Main scene and park. Run it in a GPU-backed Godot window with a fresh isolated save path, for example:

```powershell
$env:SNAPLAND_SAVE_PATH = (Join-Path (Resolve-Path '.').Path '.review\native-island-npc-motion\profile\progress.json')
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force -Path (Split-Path $env:SNAPLAND_SAVE_PATH) | Out-Null
& 'C:\Tools\Godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe' --path . res://game/tests/native_island_npc_motion_review.tscn
```

The fixture resets only that isolated profile, suppresses the arrival overlay/HUD, hides the player avatar, and waits for the scene to settle before disabling only `ParkWorld` physics. The `NpcActivity` child remains active and its normal `AnimationPlayer` route and scheduler continue. With physics frozen, the fixture places the player's camera target near each actual actor, reconstructs the same offset and look direction as the world camera, keeps its 5-unit orthographic size, and translates it parallel to the camera's own up vector to center the complete sprite frame. NPC roots and authored poses are never teleported. The fixture uses `CharacterAnimationLibrary` sequences to confirm each live `Sprite3D.texture` against source-frame image content, not the legacy four-frame baseline array.

For each capture the report stores both the projected full Sprite3D frame rectangle and the projected alpha-used silhouette rectangle. Both must fit within the 1280×720 viewport; this keeps the stronger complete-frame check while making transparent padding visible in the evidence. It also records loaded source frame counts, actor/interaction-point positions, action/direction, engine frame, and elapsed runtime. Point-follow checks allow at most 0.03 world units of same-frame animation/update ordering, and preserve the unrounded coordinates in the JSON report.

The fixture calls `NpcActivity.greet()` and `NpcActivity.interact()` directly so the authored front interactions can be reviewed consistently; those are scripted review actions, not claims of human play. Ticket and imp remain in place while each receives more than nine seconds of normal scheduled runtime, allowing the controller to choose a natural personality pose. Announcer capture phases follow the real 8.6-second activity clock through its outbound walk, stop/settle, front rest, return walk, second stop/rest, and next outbound leg. It does not seek the controller timeline or assign actor positions. At completion it checks pause freezes action time/frame/root and reduced motion restores the actor root and interaction point.

Announcer's greet and interact are timed during its regular stationary phase so the route scheduler does not replace the scripted interaction with a walk on the next frame. Ticket and imp receive their direct greet/interact review, then the fixture waits for the actual next scheduled personality event and captures it while the one-shot is live. Any failed assertion stops the process immediately and writes a failure report; this avoids continuing through later captures and producing a misleading near-pass.

Set `SNAPLAND_NATIVE_PROOF_TAG` to a fresh unique tag before each native run. The fixture sanitizes that value and writes captures under `pm/Snapland-art/images/` as `<tag>-<role>-<phase>.png`, with `<tag>-report.json` as the structured report. The historical ART-17-v4 captures are not the current prefix and are preserved. The camera remains size 5 and keeps its world orientation; its framing offset is derived from the v2 ticket bounds, shifting the camera 1.25 units above the target (1.70 units from v2's -0.45 offset). After world physics settles and is frozen, the fixture hides only the player node's visuals to prevent foreground overlap; it restores the player's prior visibility during cleanup. NPCs and their positions, activity, source frames, and interactions remain live and unchanged. Reports record that review-only player visual state. The fixture checks the full ticket frame against the 1280×720 viewport and records full/silhouette rectangles for every capture. A green fixture result means these runtime and capture contracts passed. It is not a visual-quality approval; inspect the PNGs at native resolution for face readability, complete head/feet bounds, planted feet, and the announcer's turn/stop transition.

The current island World enables all three NPC roles on short routes using measured left/right turnaround-derived source inputs with procedural lower-leg motion. The tenth Main-native review matched source frames but exposed an imp lower-body/hand cutout gap; that is historical. Root visually reviewed the eleventh review’s 21 captures across all three roles after the source/limb correction and found the former hand/hip gaps absent. This supports only the reviewed head, limb, source-frame, and route presentation checks. Authored front idle, greet, interact, emotion and role-specific secondary poses remain separate from procedural leg motion. This review does not add or imply NPC authored walk cycles or diagonal, toward-camera, or away-camera gait. The hero’s 0.600 m walk cycle and 35 mm peak lift are separate from NPC motion; source-measured foot-roll applies only to hero left/right views. NPC lift caps remain 18/20/24 mm for ticket/imp/announcer respectively. These hero parameters are not part of this NPC fixture evidence.
