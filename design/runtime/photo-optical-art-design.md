# SG-25 optical postcard treatment

## Direction

The presenter remains 2D drawing code, but the camera negative reads as a restrained **2.5D paper-cut harbor postcard**: a far headland/beacon plane, a mid-distance quay and skiffs, and a near shoreline with a few reeds. The depth comes from independently offset silhouettes, not a false claim of a 3D scene. The existing portrait, model frame, scored body bounds, shutter, badges, and three instant prints retain their geometry and mechanics.

The only parallax driver is the model snapshot's actual `frame_center`. As the player pans the viewfinder, the far shore shifts slightly against the pan and the nearer tide line shifts more. Each offset is clamped to the model's legal frame travel and kept inside `PLAY_RECT`. There is no background timer, drift, extra input, or camera motion during a stationary aim. The near shoreline starts below the maximum target-box extent, so the new foreground details do not cross the full-body subject. The harbor layers are drawn behind the existing avatar and frame.

The camera shell gets a shallow second rim and inset-glass side rails outside the scored image. This makes its optical framing apparatus more distinct from the other minigames' stages while keeping the image area open. It uses small, hand-authored vector silhouettes and the existing time-of-day palette; no new raster art or borrowed game imagery was added. Reduced-motion mode keeps the same model-linked parallax position and all capture/readiness text as a stable still.

## Reference use and originality

This work reuses the project’s documented primary-source reading rather than presenting this implementation pass as new game research. The official text sources read in the SG-25 and ART-21 records include [Adobe composition basics](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/technique/composition.html), [Adobe fill-the-frame guidance](https://lightroom.adobe.com/academy/principles/composition/fill-the-frame), [Nikon composition guidance](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/composing-photographs), [Nikon framing-assist instructions](https://www.nikonusa.com/pdf/manuals/coolpix/CP413222_en.pdf), [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 103](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103), and [PlayStation's gameplay-first UX article](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/05/11/unpacking-returnals-ux-design-gameplay-first-ui-retro-futuristic-tech-and-accessibility/). The previous ART-21 review also opened six first-party stills and read the matching page text. These sources informed subject/background separation, stable framing aids, and layered information. The cited games were not played; the six screenshots are inspected references, not gameplay evidence.

The composition is original to Snapland: one harbor camera follows the player's existing frame pan; the moving, complete-body target remains scored by the existing model; and the print rail preserves the captured pose and light. This is not a recreation of the four-view party photo layout, a copied camera interface, a static replacement background, or a newly simulated 3D camera.

## Implementation and verification

The parallax planes use the model snapshot’s `frame_center`; the view test samples their transformed bounds and confirms the portrait target box remains unchanged. The focus and score-preview labels sit on opaque deep-teal plates with the same light ink at every time phase and reduced-motion setting. The text/plate pair actually used by the renderer measures above 7:1 contrast. Tests sample the three-round target-motion envelope and verify that both plates clear the character bounds, all status badges, and the screen edges. The status row sits above the labels with a measured gap. The presenter remains 2.5D vector drawing: there is no 3D lens or depth-tested sprite.

The first native v1 review exposed a real noon-light readability failure: white focus and estimated-score text over the pale sky became hard to read. The rejected v1 images and recordings are not the final presentation. The v2 correction uses opaque dark backplates and was reviewed in the final native normal- and quarter-speed runs.

On Godot 4.7.2 headless, `test_bubble_photo_model.gd` passed 34 checks and `test_bubble_photo_view.gd` passed 101 checks. TutorialPanel/catalog and the candidate resource import passed; the panel test’s one missing screenshot warning is its deliberately invalid game-001 fixture. Final native v2 normal playback recorded 1,585 frames / 26.4167 seconds; quarter-speed playback recorded 6,052 frames / 100.8667 seconds. Both logs were error-free. Keyboard formal play scored 297 at both speeds; pointer-assisted, reduced-motion private practice scored 300 at both speeds. Each route recorded three full-body photos and exercised real pause, resume, retry, and practice. The formal-result route returned to the park; the private-practice route ended by quitting the native fixture, so a practice-to-park return is not claimed. The private-practice comparison covered `photos`, `achievements`, `quests`, `coins`, `normal_count`, `devil_count("welcome")`, `owned_clothes`, `wardrobe_rewarded_games`, and `platform_pending_achievements`. It did not establish that every setting or appearance field remained unchanged. These are native fixture and visual-review results, not a claim of independent human playthrough.

The tutorial captures use the current native daytime framing, quarter-speed exposure, and quarter-speed practice result. The practice screenshot explicitly identifies its 300 points as non-formal. The final v2 videos and stills are attached to the in-progress SG-25 ticket: [normal speed](../../pm/Snapland-small-game/images/SG-25-normal-play-v2.mp4), [quarter speed](../../pm/Snapland-small-game/images/SG-25-slow-play-v2.mp4), [daytime view](../../pm/Snapland-small-game/images/SG-25-camera-v2.png), [exposure](../../pm/Snapland-small-game/images/SG-25-exposure-v2.png), and [three-print practice view](../../pm/Snapland-small-game/images/SG-25-prints-v2.png).
