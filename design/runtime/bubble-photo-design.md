## Research read and decisions

This was a source review, not a playtest. The inspected material consists of official photography instruction, camera framing guidance, accessibility guidelines, and the project’s already-read official Mario Party reference.

## Delivered implementation and validation

The camera-framing game is integrated as game-010 with `BubblePhotoModel` and `BubblePhotoView`; Main routes keyboard and pointer input, appearance/equipment, pause, retry, practice, result, and ordinary/bonus qualification. Three actual photographs are scored from frame coverage, placement, and pose timing, with ordinary qualification at two successful shots and the bonus requiring three perfect shots. Four lighting cues affect the preview and saved print. Practice results remain isolated from persistent awards and collections.

Root's native review used real keyboard and pointer events at normal and quarter simulation speed. Formal keyboard runs scored 296/300 and 297/300; private pointer-practice runs scored 300/300 at both speeds. Pause/resume and synchronous retry-to-zero passed, and practice left photos, missions, scores, achievements, and pending private progress unchanged. The model suite passed 34 checks, the view suite 74, and the frozen Session suite 53. These are implementation and native-input QA results, not a human playtest or evidence that every first-time, failure, leave, and album flow has been accepted.

Remaining acceptance: verify the first-time tutorial and its screenshots, a genuine failed-round/result path, leaving during play and returning cleanly, and the complete earned-photo album flow. Review the final authored character animation in motion; the current view uses cached existing idle poses and must not be described as a detailed animation set.

The ticket calls the game “Bubble Photo” and asks for aiming, blowing bubbles, and shutter as a readable sequence. The delivered loop is specifically camera framing and shutter timing; it does not yet implement a bubble-blowing action. Preserve that identity with one restrained, readable bubble cue integrated into the optical framing (for example, a small soap-film focus ring that tracks focus readiness), without obscuring the full-body subject or changing the scoring rules. If the bubble action itself is required, treat it as a separate design/acceptance decision rather than implying it already exists.

Each of three rounds presents one full-body subject in a close portrait view. The player moves a fixed-size frame inside a safe play area, watches the actual subject bounding box enter it, and presses the shutter. The subject follows a deterministic, learnable path; the expression/pose has a readable best-shot interval. One shutter ends the opportunity. A successful shutter freezes a one-second photo exposure before the next round; letting the round expire records a zero-point miss, so no round can hang indefinitely.

The model's default target measures 206×298 design pixels, matching the visible-alpha union measured across the canonical front idle poses (including hair and shoes). Before a round starts, integration can pass `view.measured_subject_size()` to `model.set_subject_size()` for the currently equipped appearance; the measured silhouette cannot be changed after the first round advances or any shot is recorded. The view crops only transparent padding using the measured alpha union, then aspect-fits that body region into the target box; this keeps the scored bounds aligned with the displayed full person.

Score each photo from measured frame overlap (60 points), subject placement/centering (30), and timing quality (10), producing 0–100 continuous points. Qualification requires at least 80% of the full-body bounds, reasonable centering, and a nontrivial timing grade. A perfect photo must retain the complete body, be closely balanced in the frame, and land within the clear-pose window. Ordinary success is at least two qualified photos; the bonus requires all three perfect. The ledger records round, score, coverage, placement, pose timing, and lighting cue; these are this-run keepsakes only, not new persistent or tradable items.

`time_of_day` changes a preview/print light cue (noon warm-neutral, dusk amber, night cool lantern fill) while preserving target geometry and scoring. The implemented view uses a close, softly vignetted retro harbor camera, a restrained thirds guide, visible head/feet safety marks, clear shutter-ready shape/text, and instant-photo prints after each shot. Reduced motion keeps static readiness and progress indicators. Main/session own input routing, HUD, tutorial, pause, awards, and result integration.

## Model API and validation contract

`reset(assisted=false, time_of_day="noon")`, `set_frame_center(Vector2)`, `step(delta)`, `shutter()`, `snapshot()`. `snapshot()` reuses its state, composition, and photo containers; consumers treat them read-only. `step` clamps a single simulation advance to 0.25 seconds. Invalid centers and repeated shutter presses are harmless. Assist expands the qualification margins and pose interval but does not move the frame, trigger a shutter, or invent a capture. Day/night changes only the recorded visual treatment. Three timeouts still produce a finite result.

The `BubblePhotoView` uses a distinct close-range retro harbor camera rather than the shared minigame backdrop. It draws the model's exact viewfinder and frame, pre-caches actual `AvatarRenderer` idle poses for the current appearance/equipment, and uses the pose time recorded on each actual shot for its full-body Polaroid print. Feedback labels pair words with distinct complete/partial/cropped-frame, balanced/offset-reticle, and aperture shapes; the exposure is held static without a screen flash in reduced-motion mode. View updates retain the model snapshot and cached feedback object and allocate no avatar textures during frame drawing.

Tests cover actual movement/input/scoring for all three shots, full-body cropping, premature/repeated shutter, assistance without automation, lighting metadata, bounded time advance, timeout completion, snapshot/composition storage reuse, exact playfield/frame bounds, cached avatar alpha bounds, posed print fidelity, all four light cues, feedback semantics, and reduced-motion static states. Session wiring and native keyboard/pointer review are complete for the exercised formal, practice, pause/resume, and retry paths; remaining flows are listed above.

## View layout audit

The SG-25 native screenshots showed the camera plaque behind the persistent top HUD and the shutter caption clipped in a narrow side strip. The view now places the plaque below the HUD, keeps the exact model playfield and frame rectangles, and gives the shutter a separate 88-pixel column outside the image area. The control uses two short labels, a raised brass rim, and a slight exposure-state press; the surrounding panel adds camera depth without adding more prompts.

Status badges draw above the reticle so frame lines cannot cross their words. The score preview has a measured box separated from both those badges and the shutter. Three lower instant prints retain the captured pose and lighting, with their text constrained to each card. Layout tests measure the fallback font’s actual string widths and bounds at 1280×720, check the HUD-safe top edge, and check contrast for plaque, night readouts, and print captions. The harbor view retains its own sea, pier, and night-lantern scene; its scored playfield and full-body avatar bounds do not move or change.

Root's native v5 review confirmed the plaque is clear of the HUD, the shutter labels and score preview fit, and all three prints retain the complete captured body at both normal and quarter speed. The screenshot review is a layout check, not a human playtest or final animation review.
