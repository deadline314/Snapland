## Delivered implementation and validation

The camera-framing game is integrated as game-010 with `BubblePhotoModel` and `BubblePhotoView`; Main routes keyboard and pointer input, appearance/equipment, pause, retry, practice, result, and ordinary/bonus qualification. Three actual photographs are scored from frame coverage, placement, and pose timing, with ordinary qualification at two successful shots and the bonus requiring three perfect shots. Four lighting cues affect the preview and saved print. Practice results remain isolated from persistent awards and collections.

Root's native review used real keyboard and pointer events at normal and quarter simulation speed. Formal keyboard runs scored 296/300 and 297/300; private pointer-practice runs scored 300/300 at both speeds. Pause/resume and synchronous retry-to-zero passed, and practice left photos, missions, scores, achievements, and pending private progress unchanged. The model suite passed 34 checks, the view suite 74, and the frozen Session suite 53. These are implementation and native-input QA results, not a human playtest or evidence that every first-time, failure, leave, and album flow has been accepted.

Remaining acceptance: verify the first-time tutorial and its screenshots, a genuine failed-round/result path, leaving during play and returning cleanly, and the complete earned-photo album flow. Review the final authored character animation in motion; the current view uses cached existing idle poses and must not be described as a detailed animation set.

The accepted extension below closes the earlier gap: the fixed baseline camera loop was framing and shutter timing only; the adopted candidate adds a manually charged, launched bubble that is a real photo-qualification condition. This is a deliberate qualification change, not a claim about the prior implementation.

Each of three rounds presents one full-body subject in a close portrait view. The player moves a fixed-size frame inside a safe play area, watches the actual subject bounding box enter it, and presses the shutter. The subject follows a deterministic, learnable path; the expression/pose has a readable best-shot interval. One shutter ends the opportunity. A successful shutter freezes a one-second photo exposure before the next round; letting the round expire records a zero-point miss, so no round can hang indefinitely.

The model's default target measures 206×298 design pixels, matching the visible-alpha union measured across the canonical front idle poses (including hair and shoes). Before a round starts, integration can pass `view.measured_subject_size()` to `model.set_subject_size()` for the currently equipped appearance; the measured silhouette cannot be changed after the first round advances or any shot is recorded. The view crops only transparent padding using the measured alpha union, then aspect-fits that body region into the target box; this keeps the scored bounds aligned with the displayed full person.

Score each photo from measured frame overlap (60 points), subject placement/centering (30), and timing quality (10), producing 0–100 continuous points. Qualification requires the measured full-body rectangle to fit completely inside the camera frame and the live bubble, plus the established centering and timing thresholds. Assist can widen the bubble, centering, and timing margins, but never relaxes whole-body framing. A camera-quality photo without both full-body enclosures keeps its composition score but does not count among the two ordinary qualifying photos. A perfect photo retains the existing tight-center and clear-pose requirements and must satisfy both complete enclosures. Ordinary success remains at least two qualified photos out of three; the bonus remains all three perfect. The ledger records round, score, coverage, placement, pose timing, bubble state and lighting cue; these are this-run keepsakes only, not new persistent or tradable items.

## Adopted bubble-envelope interaction

Each six-second photo opportunity allows one manually shaped bubble. Move the existing frame as before. Holding `E` or mouse right button charges bubble size; while charging, its live circle stays attached to the frame so the player can track the portrait. Releasing locks and launches it from the shown center. A quick tap uses the accessible standard-size charge; a short hold adjusts the size, capped below one second. The frame window is 360×440 design pixels: a 300×400 measured silhouette has 30 pixels of horizontal and 20 pixels of vertical room on each side. Each avatar must still be fully inside it at shutter time. The three deterministic paths remain distinct; the third uses a slower horizontal sweep (0.45 radians per second) so the larger silhouette can be followed with real 14-pixel keyboard nudges and still has a readable envelope window. Bubble center, radius, forecast path, remaining lifetime and full-body containment are model state; the View draws that exact geometry behind the full-body subject. One predictable, per-round buoyant arc and small signaled crosswind replace random motion. Larger bubbles drift faster, with a visible charge scale and enough time to photograph the subject. The player still moves the frame and presses Space/the shutter manually. Shutters before the live “合焦可拍” cue are ignored, leave the round and score untouched, and show a brief wait message.

The judge checks all four corners of the measured full-body rectangle against the live bubble circle and uses exact rectangle enclosure for the camera. No bubble, an undersized bubble, a bubble that has drifted away or popped, or any cropped head/feet cannot qualify, even if coverage remains high. Assist broadens bubble size/center/window tolerance but never charges, launches, aims, frames or photographs for the player. Practice uses the same model rules while keeping all results private. Reduced motion holds one decorative idle frame while preserving the real readiness cue, bubble path, scale, labels and containment result. Pause and focus loss cancel an unlaunched charge so a swallowed key release cannot launch it later; already-launched bubbles remain in flight and their clocks pause with the round. Retry clears all three rounds' bubble and photo state.

Fixed implementation baseline for this adopted candidate: archive `cf719411aca2be6d15bc9a1b6a468e338095e1fb`. Acceptance for the new interaction remains pending until the new model, View, Session input, tutorial, tests and native input flow pass together.

`time_of_day` changes a preview/print light cue (noon warm-neutral, dusk amber, night cool lantern fill) while preserving target geometry and scoring. The implemented view uses a close, softly vignetted retro harbor camera, a restrained thirds guide, visible head/feet safety marks, clear shutter-ready shape/text, and instant-photo prints after each shot. Reduced motion keeps static readiness and progress indicators. Main/session own input routing, HUD, tutorial, pause, awards, and result integration.

## Model API and validation contract

The implemented candidate keeps `reset(assisted=false, time_of_day="noon")`, `set_frame_center(Vector2)`, `step(delta)`, `shutter()`, and `snapshot()`, and adds the equivalent charge-press/release bubble input. `snapshot()` reuses its state, composition, bubble and photo containers; consumers treat them read-only. `step` clamps a single simulation advance to 0.25 seconds. Invalid centers, non-finite charge time and repeated shutter/bubble inputs are harmless. Assist expands bubble containment and capture-time margins but does not move the frame, charge, launch or take a photo. Day/night changes only the recorded visual treatment. Three timeouts still produce a finite result.

The `BubblePhotoView` uses a distinct close-range retro harbor camera rather than the shared minigame backdrop. It draws the model's exact viewfinder and frame, pre-caches actual `AvatarRenderer` idle poses for the current appearance/equipment, and uses the pose time recorded on each actual shot for its full-body Polaroid print. Feedback labels pair words with distinct complete/partial/cropped-frame, balanced/offset-reticle, and aperture shapes; the exposure is held static without a screen flash in reduced-motion mode. View updates retain the model snapshot and cached feedback object and allocate no avatar textures during frame drawing.

Baseline tests cover actual movement/input/scoring for all three shots, full-body cropping, premature/repeated shutter, assistance without automation, lighting metadata, bounded time advance, timeout completion, snapshot/composition storage reuse, exact playfield/frame bounds, cached avatar alpha bounds, posed print fidelity, all four light cues, feedback semantics, and reduced-motion static states. New candidate tests additionally cover true bubble containment, no-bubble/undersized/off-subject/camera-cropped failures, natural three-shot qualification and perfect results, equivalent keyboard/pointer charge, timeouts, pause/retry, practice isolation and reduced-motion truth. Native acceptance for the new interaction is not claimed until real keyboard and mouse routes pass.

## View layout audit

The SG-25 native screenshots showed the camera plaque behind the persistent top HUD and the shutter caption clipped in a narrow side strip. The view now places the plaque below the HUD, keeps the exact model playfield and frame rectangles, and gives the shutter a separate 88-pixel column outside the image area. The control uses two short labels, a raised brass rim, and a slight exposure-state press; the surrounding panel adds camera depth without adding more prompts.

Status badges draw above the reticle so frame lines cannot cross their words. The score preview has a measured box separated from both those badges and the shutter. Three lower instant prints retain the captured pose and lighting, with their text constrained to each card. Layout tests measure the fallback font’s actual string widths and bounds at 1280×720, check the HUD-safe top edge, and check contrast for plaque, night readouts, and print captions. The harbor view retains its own sea, pier, and night-lantern scene; its scored playfield and full-body avatar bounds do not move or change.

Root's native v5 review confirmed the plaque is clear of the HUD, the shutter labels and score preview fit, and all three prints retain the complete captured body at both normal and quarter speed. The screenshot review is a layout check, not a human playtest or final animation review.


## SG-25 final native evidence and tutorial behavior

The final bubble-envelope candidate was exercised through the isolated native fixture at normal and quarter speed. Both videos reached their natural end: 1,590 frames / 26.500 seconds and 6,057 frames / 100.950 seconds at 60 fps. The formal keyboard route produced 295 in both runs (three full-body perfect prints, with individual results 99, 98, 98); the pointer, assisted, reduced-motion private route produced 296. Root watched both videos to completion and reviewed all 30 captured states in eight contact sheets. This is native fixture evidence, not a claim of full human playtesting or final art approval. The current first-visit tutorial was reviewed in the native Main flow: Root clicked both real next-page buttons, verified all three mapped 1280x720 textures, and visually inspected the captures. Its 35-frame, 60 fps panel review exited cleanly with no engine errors or resource leaks. This is tutorial-panel evidence, not a gameplay movie or full human playtest.

The player-facing sequence now follows the actual interaction: first place the frame and manually charge/release one bubble; then wait for the bubble and frame to contain the whole body and for the focus cue before taking the one permitted shot; finally compare all three print scores and the ordinary/devil qualification. The result example is the observed 99/98/98 (295 total), not a perfect 300. The three lesson images are actual formal keyboard captures: charge, launched envelope with readiness cues, and the three-print result.

Two earlier fixture runs failed on fixture defects, not game behavior: the first inverted the boolean check for whether the photo list was empty; the second read the held-E charge state before flushing the buffered input. The corrected fixture kept the actual behavior assertions, and both final gameplay speeds completed with zero engine errors.
