# Minigame presentation diversity survey

**Scope:** design research for presentation only. This document does not change mechanics, scoring, `Session`, `Main`, or assets. The shared park identity stays harbor-side: sea-glass teal, coral, ochre, warm paper, and dark ink. What varies is the framing, depth, scale, and visual rhythm that make each mechanic legible. These are original Snapland directions, not proposals to reproduce a cited game's art.

## What the current game actually does

I read all twelve entries in `game/data/welcome_games.json` and the `game/minigames/session.gd` draw dispatch. The definitions describe twelve distinct verbs: fly-and-brake deliveries; steer a kite through rings; time jumps to a fountain; route crossing crowds; catch hats; follow dance beats; rotate pipe tiles; stabilize a sign; align a cast shadow; frame bubble portraits; swap seats; and repeat a melody.

The code has a separate `_draw_*` function for each verb, but every function is staged inside the same 1280×720 design frame, double ink/brass border, rivets, paper mat, central backdrop crop and bottom ground strip. Backdrops collapse into four repeated harbor/garden/stage/night scenes, with those modes sharing the same awning, curtain, or moon-and-lantern dressing. So mechanics differ more than their visual presentation. The proposal below keeps one island palette and changes the camera and staging only where that clarifies what the player is doing.

### Shared constraints, deliberately kept

- Keep the district theme recognizable through harbor materials, palette, and local objects, while changing composition, camera, depth, and key motion by mechanic. A common frame should not require identical frame size, backdrop crop, or bottom band.
- Put the interactive object in the camera's natural focal area: ring ahead of kite, junction grid for pipes, sign pivot for balance, shadow wall for alignment. Keep interaction targets separated from instruction text and timer/score.
- Make state transitions causal: water flows after connection; the crossing clears after safe routing; the camera prints only after a valid shot. Do not add decorative movement that competes with the input cue.
- Preserve legibility at reduced motion. Every animated cue needs a stable counterpart: highlighted next ring, static brightest fountain phase, lit note pad, or filled hold segment.
- Vary rendering medium deliberately: flat diagram for logic, orthographic surface for routes/seating, side-plane staging for flight/catching/shadows, and limited 3D perspective for fountain/sign/photo moments that benefit from depth. This is a design menu, not a request for twelve unrelated asset pipelines.

## First-party references actually read

I read the listed page text and the quoted sections below. This was desk research: I did not play the cited games, watch embedded trailers, or download assets. Official source pages exposed screenshots as image links; attempts to open those links returned URL metadata or fetch errors rather than inspectable pixels. Therefore the design recommendations above derive from the written descriptions and the current game code, not visual screenshot analysis.

1. **Nintendo, [WarioWare: Get It Together!](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/warioware-get-it-together-switch/), lines 135–145, 149–230.** The page describes a collection of very short comic microgames, but also gives player characters sharply different movement/attack abilities. Adopted principle: a shared collection wrapper does not require each interaction to reuse one avatar pose or camera; here, each minigame should be composed around its distinct action verb. Not adopted: frantic cuts or character swaps—the Snapland games are longer and calmer.
2. **Nintendo, [Mario Party Superstars, 1-vs-3 minigames](https://mariopartysuperstars.nintendo.com/minigames/1-vs-3/), search-result body read; page-open timed out.** Its official list gives different spatial verbs such as dodge falling coconuts, aim spotlights, kick/defend, and steer around holes. Adopted principle: gameplay staging should teach roles and spatial relationships at a glance. I did not infer an art or camera rule from the inaccessible images.
3. **Panic / Playdate Catalog, [b360](https://play.date/games/b360/), page text lines 31–46 and game pitch in search result.** The game is pitched as brick-breaking with a rotational twist and explicitly lists D-pad/crank plus quick reflexes. Adopted principle: a signature control can define a signature motion path; Snapland can let the wind, string, light beam, water, or timeline itself carry the visual feedback.
4. **Annapurna Interactive, [Donut County](https://annapurnainteractive.com/games/donut-county), lines 28–33.** The publisher describes a physics puzzle played as an expanding hole that swallows objects and environments. Adopted principle: let the playfield's physical rule create the spectacle and progression rather than lay the same decoration behind every round. Not adopted: its hole/consumption concept.
5. **Drool, [Thumper](https://thumpergame.com/), lines 40–50.** The creators describe a rhythm-action game framed as a space beetle travelling through a hellish void and state that the presentation works in glossy 2D or VR. Adopted principle: tempo, speed, and camera intimacy should correspond to the rhythm verb; Snapland's dance stage and bell-memory instrument need distinct rhythm staging. Not adopted: aggressive color, violence, or continuous forward track.
6. **Enhance, [Lumines Arise](https://lumines.game/), lines 27–31, 123–135; [game modes and accessibility](https://lumines.game/about-the-game/), lines 57–69 and 121–123.** The official page connects a tile-matching rule to a beat-sweeping timeline and different stage environments; the options text mentions reducing effects/motion to improve readability. Adopted principle: synchronize visual change to the mechanic, and plan reduced-motion behavior as a legibility mode, not as a missing effect.
7. **Lucas Pope / 3909, [Return of the Obra Dinn prerelease notes](https://obradinn.com/media/prerelease.html), lines 21–41.** The creator describes a first-person mystery/deduction game rendered at fixed low resolution with 1-bit dithering and gives alternate output settings because long sessions may be uncomfortable. Adopted principle: camera, display treatment, accessibility and duration form one coherent decision; a distinct rendering mode should serve the kind of observation the mechanic asks for. Not adopted: one-bit rendering or its fixed resolution.
8. **House House / Panic, [Untitled Goose Game](https://untitled.goose.game/), lines 4–14; [official gameplay trailer description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wQknBJo4aBQ) read as text only.** The official site gives the goose's point of view in one sentence and links its screenshot set; the trailer description calls it a slapstick stealth sandbox with object pranks in village spaces. Adopted principle: one clear playable silhouette plus a few interactive props can carry a whole scene, and the space should be shaped around the player's mischief. I did not watch the trailer or inspect its stills.
9. **Lucas Pope, [Mars After Midnight](https://play.date/games/mars-after-midnight/), lines 23–42 and 54–58.** Its official catalog entry presents a concrete work fantasy—screening entrants, tidying, and scheduling sessions—and describes the crank as intentionally imprecise. Adopted principle: match visual precision to input tolerance; avoid tiny indicators for broad continuous movement. Not adopted: its office setting or monochrome handheld presentation.

## Implementation priorities for the runtime owner

1. **First split composition from mechanics.** Prototype three very different layouts—`game-004` overhead crossing, `game-007` pipe plan, and `game-010` camera booth—using existing primitives/assets if possible. These expose whether the current one-frame layout is constraining unrelated mechanics. Keep HUD and input unchanged.
2. **Separate camera systems before producing twelve backdrops.** Establish small presentation families with per-game composition data: side plane, overhead board, frontal stage, close-up prop portrait, and miniature diorama. Let individual scenes opt into a family and preserve their play-space coordinates; do not implement arbitrary post-process tint changes as diversity.
3. **Then build three signature feedback moments.** Fountain's brightest-beat jump, sign's settling pivot, and bubble camera's printed result each have a naturally different payoff. Make each success animation causally tied to its existing state, provide a reduced-motion static form, and validate that UI never covers the action/readable target.

No runtime tests, GPU captures, or visual asset inspection were performed for this survey.
