# Daylight, local lights, and paper-theatre backgrounds

Research performed on 2026-10-12 before implementation. The sources below were opened and the cited sections read; these are primary engine or platform/developer sources.

## Read sources and decisions

| Primary source and observed guidance | Adopted or rejected for Snapland |
|---|---|
| [Godot DirectionalLight3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_directionallight3d.html): a directional light is parallel rays; its transform basis controls direction, not its position. Longer shadow distance reduces detail and increases work. | Adopt the canonical profile's key-light rotation; do not animate its origin. Keep shadow distance limited to the island and fade beyond the camera-visible play space. |
| [Godot 3D lights and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html): directional lights are inexpensive for scene lighting but shadow maps cost more; angular-size softening (PCSS) is expensive. Positional lights share a finite shadow atlas. | Keep one directional key. Avoid PCSS on the key and avoid enabling shadows on every lantern. Prefer a few selected local lights without shadows for warm pools. |
| [Godot OmniLight3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_omnilight3d.html): lights attenuate over a finite radius; Mobile and Compatibility impose a per-mesh visible-light cap. | Bound simultaneously active lanterns and give them short ranges. The visible lantern mesh/emission remains when its OmniLight is off. |
| [Godot Light3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_light3d.html): realtime shadowing has a significant performance cost; use it only where it changes the scene noticeably. Bias and blur address mapping artifacts but can separate or grain shadows. | Do not cast realtime shadows from billboard actors or alpha-cut foliage. A face-on billboard can create a camera-facing card shadow and/or self-shadow edge. Keep the true directional shadow on fixed 3D architecture. |
| [Godot SpriteBase3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_spritebase3d.html): Sprite3D shading is opt-in; billboard, two-sided rendering, texture filtering, and shadow casting are separate concerns. Nearest texture filtering affects sampling, not shadow resolution. | Keep character textures shaded by the phase key but turn off their card shadow. Keep leaf Sprite3Ds shaded while preventing them from casting a second, view-dependent shadow. |
| [Godot Environment](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_environment.html): one Environment controls background, ambient lighting, fog, and post-processing; glow and tonemapping occur late in the environment pipeline. | Apply all four canonical phase values to the shared scene Environment, including fog/ambient rather than using a large brightness-only multiplier. Keep glow conservative at dusk/night. |
| [Godot 2D lights and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html): 2D shadow quality is tied to Viewport pixel resolution, not source sprite texels; nearest sampling alone does not make pixel shadows. Pixel snapping requires changing light/shadow vertex positions in a custom shader. | Reject a new realtime Light2D per minigame actor. Use a restrained phase tint over the existing four backdrop themes, below the actors and interaction prompts; this preserves contrast and avoids pretending the CanvasItem layer receives the 3D key light. |
| [Epic's Acquire interview on Octopath Traveler II](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/octopath-traveler-ii-builds-a-bigger-bolder-world-in-its-stunning-hd-2d-style?lang=en-US): the team describes pixel characters over 3D environments, a smaller head/body ratio, and dynamic time lighting, noting the integration required iteration. | Adopt the small set of legible, tested time treatments and consistent key direction; do not copy game content or assume billboard faces will physically catch 3D illumination without testing. |
| [Nintendo's Yoshi's Crafted World page](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Yoshi-s-Crafted-World-1233953.html): describes authored front/back views exposing everyday materials, and exploration that uses both foreground and background. | Keep the existing paper cut-out backdrops and their four authored scenes; use a light color veil rather than replacing art or obscuring functional play layers. No Nintendo art or assets are used. |

## Implementation decisions

- `TimeOfDay` is the single canonical data source for `dawn`, `noon`, `dusk`, and `night`; the world and minigame session accept stable phase IDs and normalize through `TimeOfDay.phase_for_id()`.
- The island uses its existing one directional sun/moon. Phase profiles set rotation, key color/energy, sky color, ambient color/energy, and a related low-density fog tint together.
- Real 3D shadows remain on stationary geometry. Billboard tree, shrub, and character cards do not cast directional shadows; small grounded 3D contact-shadow discs provide stable foot contact and remain separate from sprite shading. This deliberately gives up exact foliage silhouettes because the authored leaf atlas already carries internal highlights/shadows that a second cutout shadow would double.
- Night/dusk warmth comes from a small, bounded set of actual OmniLights; the bulbs remain visible in daytime. Local lights do not cast shadows, so there is no all-lantern shadow-atlas churn. Reduced-motion settings do not disable required lighting.
- All twelve minigames retain their existing harbor/garden/stage/night art and game-space geometry. A low-opacity phase wash is drawn over the backdrop before gameplay actors, targets, and text; no state, hitbox, marker, or rule depends on the chosen phase.

## Limits and validation

The sprite/leaf atlas is a 2D card with artwork that includes painted shading. The result can be phase-tinted and receive the key light as a shaded Sprite3D, but its painted highlight direction cannot be physically rotated with the 3D sun; the asset has no normal map. Realtime alpha-card shadows would be view-dependent and would conflict with its baked shading, so those are intentionally not claimed as physically exact. Visual appearance still requires native GPU capture; headless checks only verify API wiring, profile selection, and safe ranges.


## 2026-10-23 Measured light/shadow agreement (ART-13)

A native review (`game/tests/native_light_occlusion_review.tscn -- --lighting`) now measures each caster's shadow in the real Main scene for all four phases instead of judging screenshots only. For one caster at a time it silences every other shadow caster, renders OFF/ON/OFF frames (only pixels that are identical in both OFF frames count, so animated water/flags drop out), keeps the shadow region connected to the caster's base, and compares its centroid direction with the projected key-light (or lamp) direction. Casters: the hero and three NPC actor proxies, a tree, a lamp post, a planter, a shrub and a kiosk.

Defects it found and the fixes:

- **The lawn ignored the sun.** The island lawn fan was wound so its top was a back face; with `cull_disabled` Godot flipped the normal downward. Grass therefore received no key light and every cast shadow vanished on lawns (a tree's trunk shadow appeared to start ~55px from its base where the paving began). The fan now runs centre → current rim → next rim (front face up) with back-face culling, and the lawn albedo was halved so the sunlit meadow keeps its authored tone. `test_daylight_lighting` checks every lawn triangle faces up and rejects the old order as a counter-example.
- **Painted lamp posts, planters and shrubs cast no shadow** while trees and actors did. Each now owns hidden `SHADOWS_ONLY` volumes sized from its painted silhouette (`SPRITE_SHADOW_VOLUMES` in `park_world.gd`): pole/lantern/arm/bed, pot/foliage, dome. Grounded parts are checked never to hover above the ground; none adds collision.
- **Dawn lamps were almost invisible** (weakest ground pool +1.5 luma at 0.18 energy). Dawn lamp energy is 0.32: visible but well below dusk 0.48 / night 0.82. Noon lamps stay off.

Accepted limits: only the two lamps nearest the player cast lamp shadows (bounded shadow atlas); an actor beyond 70% of such a lamp's range is not expected to show a lamp shadow. Painted highlights on billboards are fixed art and do not rotate with the sun. All evidence is Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility on llvmpipe (software GL), not Forward+, Windows hardware or a human playtest.
