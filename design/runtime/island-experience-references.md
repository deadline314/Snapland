# Island park experience references

Research note for the island park redesign. Sources below distinguish direct observations from design interpretation. Bomberman Land 3 (BL3) media and guide pages are archival/secondary references; screenshots are not developer commentary. The recommendations are principles to reinterpret for Snapland, not layouts, art, dialogue, or UI to copy.

## Reference set and observations

A large numbered grid overlays the right side. It is a useful warning too: the overlay makes the experience read like a selection menu laid over a park, so keep exploration feedback situated in-world where possible. Screenshot does not establish how the park is traversed or how the overlay behaves.

Its key lesson is attraction-specific staging and readable, local rules; do not reproduce its exact tower, UI, or assets.

4. **Bomberman Land 3 exploration / progression** — [GameFAQs user-authored Piece Locations Guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/925986-bomberman-land-3/faqs/39323). **Guide read; secondary player documentation.** It names materially different places such as a pedestrian bridge, lighthouse, fountain plaza, casino, beach/cave, restaurant, and mountain. It describes finding pieces by clearing attractions, talking to NPCs, returning after a line disappears, and asking a character about a clue. This supports the reference to multiple landmarks and people as the attraction fabric; it does not prove a particular dialogue cadence or that every character has regular dialogue.

5. **Square Enix, HD-2D art and world design** — [“9 things we learned from Inside DRAGON QUEST III HD-2D Remake”](https://www.square-enix-games.com/en_GB/news/inside-dragon-quest-iii-hd-2d-remake) (official Square Enix editorial drawing on a developer interview). **Read directly.** The team describes tuning color, lighting, depth effects and water to the game’s own tone; settlements get distinct palettes, flags and lighting; elevation in the overworld was used to make travel feel adventurous. **Principle:** build the park with a consistent pixel/3D visual grammar, then give each attraction landmark its own silhouette, palette, height and environmental motion so wayfinding comes from place identity. Do not clone DQ/Octopath assets or claim the HD-2D label implies one fixed camera/render recipe.

6. **Square Enix, HD-2D camera/depth** — [The Adventures of Elliot developer interview](https://www.jp.square-enix.com/column/detail/Elliot-interview0806/) (official Square Enix interview; Japanese). **Read directly.** Producer Naofumi Matsushita describes a mostly fixed overhead camera and a “drum-roll” undulating depth treatment in the field to bring out a different kind of space; the interview also notes simple controls paired with exploration rewards. **Principle:** use controlled camera framing and layered depth to reveal the next attraction, while preserving legibility and simple navigation. This is one team’s implementation, not a universal HD-2D requirement.

7. **Universal Studios Japan, park play** — [SUPER NINTENDO WORLD Play Experience](https://www.usj.co.jp/web/en/us/areas/super-nintendo-world/play-experience) (official current park page). **Read directly.** It describes physical ? Blocks, rhythm congas that call Rambi, hidden marks that reveal characters/items, binocular lookouts, and optional Power-Up Band challenges; score/stamps can be checked through the app or at checkpoints, and the page says the app is not required to enjoy the land. **Principle:** let scenery invite small interactions and secrets; make the optional collection layer reward exploration without making a companion screen necessary. Adapt as fictional island interactions, not literal Mario props or paid-band progression.

8. **Universal Studios Japan, attraction mix and world staging** — [2020 official launch press release PDF](https://www.usj.co.jp/company/company_e/news/2020/pdf/1130_e.pdf). **PDF read directly.** The release describes a multilevel area with landmark castles over the land, a mix of rides, food, shops and other experiences, and physical scenery plus AR/projection for the Mario Kart story. **Principle:** combine traversal, attractions and social/rest spaces; use vertical landmarks to make the park readable. This is corporate promotional material and an announced experience, so it is not independent evaluation of guest outcomes.

9. **Nintendo, photo loop** — [New Pokémon Snap official product page](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/new-pokemon-snap-110735/). **Read directly.** It describes observing unexpected behavior across distinct environments, completing a Photodex, then returning to an in-game album/Re-Snap workflow for brightness, blur, zoom, stickers, frames and filters, with sharing and reactions. **Principle:** make the camera an exploration tool and the album a satisfying second creative space; celebrate expressive/rare moments, not only a single “correct” score. For Snapland, root framing, stickers and keepsakes in the island’s own visitor culture and UI language rather than copying Pokémon’s Photodex or scoring.

10. **Professional accessibility guidance for dialogue/audio** — [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 104: Subtitles and captions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/104) (Microsoft Learn). **Read directly.** It says NPC chatter/dialogue can carry story and task information; subtitles should cover speech, identify speakers, and be available before audio is missed; important non-speech sounds should have captions or visual equivalents, with spatial indication when useful. **Principle:** recurring character dialogue can have a light, recognizable voice/beep identity, but its meaning must remain available as readable text; offer subtitle/audio controls and caption meaningful cues. Beeps are a style choice, not a substitute for speech access.

11. **Professional UI design reference** — [GDC Vault: “Cutting Apart The Diegetic Interface of Hardspace: Shipbreaker”](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027158/Cutting-Apart-The-Diegetic-Interface). **Session description read; full talk appears access-gated, not viewed.** The talk frames interface design as combining diegetic and conventional approaches to preserve presence while retaining usability. **Principle:** make the camera/album feel like a park souvenir tool, but allow clear, fast controls and readable framing guidance. Diegesis should support usability, not obscure it.

12. **Unreal Engine, Octopath Traveler II world construction** — [Developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/octopath-traveler-ii-builds-a-bigger-bolder-world-in-its-stunning-hd-2d-style?lang=en-US). **Interview read directly; embedded video not watched.** The developers describe detailed 3D environments behind pixel characters, day/night variation, and the constraint of keeping attractive destinations visible within a fixed-camera composition. **Principle:** treat each island attraction as a readable landmark in the camera’s actual view, and use lighting/material depth to support pixel characters without crowding their silhouettes.

13. **Unreal Engine, Octopath Traveler visual-style development** — [Acquire interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/octopath-traveler-s-hd-2d-art-style-and-story-make-for-a-jrpg-dream-come-true). **Article read directly; embedded video not watched.** The developers describe combining 2D pixel art with a 3D environment and iterating toward more convincing light and shadow. **Principle:** the layered look depends on intentional lighting and material response, not merely placing pixel billboards over primitive geometry. This is a craft reference, not a fixed recipe Snapland should imitate.

14. **Godot 3D Environment and post-processing** — [Godot 4.2 documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html). **Read directly.** Environment ambient lighting and exposure are independent controls affecting scene brightness and contrast. **Principle:** calibrate the active time-of-day environment and actual material albedo together; a construction-time light value is not sufficient if the runtime preset later replaces it.

15. **Godot StandardMaterial3D** — [Godot 4.0 documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.0/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html). **Read directly.** Albedo color modulates a texture and roughness controls surface reflection. **Principle:** use textured, appropriately rough coastal surfaces with restrained tint, and inspect the result under the runtime lighting preset.

## Selected redesign principles

- Treat the park as a walkable composition of distinctive attractions: vary footprint, height, silhouette, approach and surrounding activity. Avoid a row of interchangeable booths or a repeated grid. Give the player sightlines and environmental landmarks that make choosing where to go pleasurable.
- Make each attraction feel like a small place with a theme, a readable entry, a character or attendant, and a short activity loop. Mix scale and rhythm: a fountain plaza, cave, lighthouse overlook, hillside course, quiet garden, market, or moving ride can coexist without every stop being a minigame kiosk.
- Populate the island with characters worth approaching. Give each a name/recognizable voice cue and small, regular conversational updates tied to time, discoveries or park progress. Keep dialogue optional and skippable; represent all meaningful speech in text, and caption important sounds. Avoid forcing exhaustive NPC checklists.
- Use the camera as an invitation to notice: interesting silhouettes, candid character moments, layered vistas and small visual discoveries. Let the player frame freely, save favorite shots, and decorate/share from an album with playful, diegetic affordances. Keep the workflow quick and legible, with basic options outside the fiction if needed.
- Let the environment teach: color, props, sound, queues, sightlines and character behavior can point toward activities without relying on a permanent HUD. An optional park journal/map can orient players who want explicit direction.
- Keep the HD-2D reference at the level of craft: deliberate pixel/3D layering, depth, lighting and distinct palettes for places, while choosing a Snapland-specific palette, forms, camera and interface.

Root also read Adobe walk-cycle article sections contact/passing/down/up and Spine ghosting and IK guide: pose continuity and planted-foot timing need inspection against ground travel. More frames alone do not verify a natural gait.

16. **Godot stable Environment / SSAO** — [Official environment and post-processing guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html). Root read the SSAO section directly on 2026-10-13: radius and intensity need restraint; AO primarily affects ambient light, screen-space occluders must remain visible, and Compatibility has a simplified radius/intensity implementation since 4.6 (Mobile does not support SSAO). We use conservative contact depth alongside actual sun shadows, and verify the native view separately. A billboard tree now has hidden trunk/canopy shadow volumes rather than a rectangular billboard shadow; this is our implementation choice, not a claim that the documentation prescribed it.

17. **Godot CameraAttributesPractical** — [Official camera reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_cameraattributespractical.html). Root read the property descriptions directly: the far focus distance is measured from the camera, the transition ramps blur gradually, blur should stay low, and DOF works in Forward+ / Mobile but not Compatibility or transparent viewports. Any focus treatment must preserve near-player readability; merely adding blur does not establish HD-2D quality.

18. **Godot Polygon2D** — [Official API reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_polygon2d.html). Root read texture/UV, skeleton and bone-weight properties and methods directly. A continuous textured polygon can be driven by per-vertex bone weights; this supports an isolated gait prototype with a continuous knee surface. The API does not certify anatomy, identity or natural motion; those remain separate native review criteria.
